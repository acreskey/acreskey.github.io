Sources, definitions & important caveats

Primary source: World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI), including series produced by ILO, UNESCO, IMF and national statistical agencies. This is a static HTML file: on opening, your browser fetches WDI observations from the World Bank public API and attempts the IMF DataMapper API for general-government debt; if that API is blocked, it displays a small bundled set of historical IMF reference observations instead. China augmented debt uses the published 2025 IMF Article IV table (2026 staff report). No account, server, or API key required. An internet connection is required, including to load Chart.js.

Labour-force participation includes both employed people and unemployed people seeking work. GDP per capita uses constant international dollars at PPP, not market-exchange-rate USD. Government debt uses IMF World Economic Outlook general-government gross debt, which includes more than central-government debt. IMF projections can appear for future years when the live API is available. The offline debt fallback contains only 2024 observations for all countries and 2020–2023 additional observations for China, sourced from the IMF via FRED; it is not a complete time series. China augmented debt is an additional IMF Article IV staff estimate that includes certain off-budget activities and local-government financing vehicles (LGFVs); it is not directly comparable to the standard general-government debt series. Interest rates are real bank lending rates, not central-bank policy rates. Household consumption per capita is a proxy for household material living standards—not disposable income; no internationally consistent disposable-income series for all seven countries is included.

Compare with care: availability differs by series and country. Some reported figures are estimates; methodologies, survey coverage, and breaks can differ, notably for China and Russia. Missing observations are never filled or invented, and the date of the newest observation can vary by several years. IMF gross debt and China augmented debt have different perimeters, and projected values are not historical observations. If a series is empty, visit the source link to investigate missingness.

Further reading: Our World in Data provides excellent explanatory charts and downloadable data; OECD Data Explorer is preferable for household net adjusted disposable income for its covered countries; IMF is useful for government debt and monetary indicators.
